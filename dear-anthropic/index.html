Open letter / Head of Search & Knowledge

Dear Anthropic.

The rise of Google taught us that searching is thinking.

For Claude to become a trusted daily habit, search and knowledge retrieval must be treated as first-order product surfaces. But great search is not just a model problem or a retrieval problem. It is an ecosystem problem, shaped by publisher incentives, access, commerce infrastructure, and the changing economics of the web.

I’ve spent my career working inside that ecosystem. I want to help Anthropic build the most trusted search experience in the world.

01 / The shift

Search is the scaffolding we use to navigate knowledge.

It is also one of the web’s deepest habits. Google now handles more than 5 trillion searches a year through an interface that feels familiar, trusted, and effortless.

But great search was never just a ranking problem. In Delphic Costs and Benefits in Web Search, Broder and McAfee argue that search quality is really about reducing the total cognitive, interaction, and time costs required to reach a useful outcome. That is the right frame for AI, too. Users do not care whether retrieval was technically elegant. They care whether the system helps them get somewhere useful, quickly, clearly, and with enough confidence to act.

That distinction matters because benchmark performance is not the same as product value. BrowseComp tests whether a system can locate a single hard-to-find fact, and its creators explicitly note that it is not designed to measure common open-ended queries. DeepSearchQA measures something different again: exhaustive, multi-step research across open-ended search spaces. Both are useful. Neither is the same as daily search, where the job is often simpler and harder at once: reduce the costs of deciding. Help me decide faster, with less effort, in the right format, and with enough trust to move.

This is why search matters so much now. It is the surface where models earn trust, become habit, and shape a user’s relationship with reality.

02 / The wedge

The wedge is trust, earned through habit.

Anthropic has said Claude should be “a trusted tool for thought.” But trust like that is rarely granted all at once. It is built through repeated success on smaller, everyday questions, the moments that teach users when a system is useful, when it is reliable, and when it is worth returning to.

That is why search matters strategically. If Claude is going to be the system people trust for questions that are consequential, confusing, or expensive, it first has to become the place they turn when the stakes are low. Habit is what builds the trust that later matters under pressure.

The wedge, then, is not generic assistant utility. It is becoming the default starting point for everyday uncertainty, and using that repeated contact to earn the right to help when the stakes are real.

This is a particularly strong wedge for Anthropic. Claude already signals care, caution, and seriousness more credibly than most AI products. Search can turn those qualities into a durable advantage by making research feel calm, legible, and dependable.

If Anthropic gets this right, it does not just gain more usage. It becomes the system people trust when the answer actually matters.

03 / The information ecosystem

Search sits on top of the information ecosystem.

Great search does not begin with the model. It begins with the information ecosystem the model sits on top of. If the underlying supply of information is weak, inaccessible, poorly attributed, or structurally incomplete, the search experience will be weaker too.

The first layer is access. The BBC’s research on AI assistants is useful here because it shows that quality problems are often inseparable from sourcing problems. If assistants cannot reliably access high-quality information, or if they present it without enough attribution, context, or clarity, trust breaks down quickly. Search quality depends on the health of the information supply chain, not just the intelligence of the interface.

The second layer is structure. Many of the most valuable search experiences do not depend on prose alone. They depend on machine-readable data: product specs, pricing, reviews, merchant feeds, availability, opening hours, maps, and local business information. This is what turns search from a text experience into a useful one. It is also what underpins agentic commerce, local discovery, and many of the workflows where AI can actually help people do something rather than just read something.

That is why building a great search experience requires grappling with both access and structure. Access determines whether the system can reach trustworthy information in the first place. Structure determines whether it can turn that information into something useful, legible, and actionable.

In other words: search is not just a model problem or a retrieval problem. It is an ecosystem problem.

04 / The UX of search

People do not experience search as a text box. They experience it as a rich results environment.

User expectations were trained by modern search engines, not by chat alone. People expect images, videos, maps, recipes, product panels, local results, and other result types that help them choose the right mode of attention quickly. A strong search experience is multimodal and situational by default.

That matters because format is part of trust. A recipe query should not look like a market research query. A local intent query should surface place data and availability. A how-to query may need video or stepwise structure. The interface should signal what kind of knowledge is being returned, not just paraphrase everything into the same block of prose.

Anthropic should treat this as a UX discipline, not a garnish layer. The winner in search will not just have the best model underneath. It will know when to answer with text, when to show a map, when to foreground an image, and when to preserve the source format because that is what the user actually needs to decide.

05 / Metrics, evals, provenance

Search quality should be measured like a trust system, not just a relevance system.

The usual search metrics still matter: coverage, freshness, latency, answer relevance, abandonment, task completion. But AI search adds a more important layer. You need evals for confidence calibration, source diversity, citation usefulness, update sensitivity, and whether users can meaningfully inspect why an answer should be believed.

This is where provenance stops being a UI garnish and becomes a product discipline. The ideal experience does not merely attach links after the fact. It surfaces evidence in the rhythm of the interaction, with enough clarity that users can inspect, compare, and continue thinking.

A serious search organization at Anthropic would treat evals, provenance design, and knowledge partnerships as one operating system. The point is not to maximize visible citations. The point is to make truth-seeking behavior easier than bluffing.

06 / Why me

I sit at the intersection of search strategy, knowledge systems, and product judgment.

My angle is not just SEO, not just product, and not just editorial. It is the connective tissue between how information is discovered, how it is structured, how people trust it, and how organizations turn that into a differentiated user experience.

I think in wedges, interfaces, supply dynamics, incentives, and operating systems. I know how to build narratives internally, translate strategic ambiguity into programs, and make search legible across product, partnerships, policy, and go-to-market functions.

For this role, the job is not merely improving retrieval quality. It is helping Anthropic define what trusted knowledge looks like as a product category and then organizing the company around it.

07 / First 90 days

Start with the product truth, then build the operating system behind it.

The first 90 days should not begin with a grand reorg or an abstract strategy deck. They should begin with product truth: where Claude search is already strong, where it breaks trust, what user behaviors are forming, and which strategic bets are being underexploited.

08 / Closing statement

Anthropic has a credible path to become the trusted layer between people and the world they need to understand.

That path runs through search. Not search as ten blue links. Not search as a backend connector. Search as a deliberate knowledge product: careful retrieval, legible evidence, calm confidence, and repeatable trust.

If the thesis is right, then this role is not support for the roadmap. It is part of defining the roadmap. I’d like to help Anthropic build that category.
